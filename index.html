The questions people actually ask.

Mostly about the catch. There is not one, but that is exactly what someone with a catch would say, so here is the detail.

I never asked for this. Why are you texting me?

Because your number is published for people to ring, and we think a page would earn its keep for you. There is nothing built and nothing owed — the message is an offer and a form, and if you fill the form in we make a start. If you would rather we did not, reply STOP and that is the last you hear from us.

What does it cost?

One payment, once, for the design and the build. No retainer, no monthly fee, nothing that renews. We quote the number when we send you the finished page, so you are looking at the real thing and the real price at the same time rather than agreeing to one before you have seen the other. Filling in the form costs nothing and is not an order.

What if I fill in the form and then change my mind?

Then you say no and that is the end of it. You have not paid anything, there is no account to close and nothing to cancel. The page comes down and we delete what you sent us.

If I stop paying you, does my site disappear?

There is nothing to stop paying. The domain, the hosting and the enquiry form are all opened in your name, with you setting the password on each. If you never speak to us again the site stays up, because none of it runs through us.

Can I change it later, or am I stuck with you?

You own the files and the accounts, so you can change it yourself, ask us, or hand it to somebody else entirely. We are not holding anything you would have to come back for.

Why only one page? Everyone else sells five.

Most people arrive on a phone, having searched your trade and your suburb. They want to know who you are, how to reach you, when you are open and whether you are real. A second page is where those four answers go to hide. One page that answers them beats five that bury them.

I already have a website.

Then the question is whether it does those four things on a phone, quickly. If it does, keep it — we will say so. If it is slow, hard to change, or costing you every month for something you cannot edit, that is the case for replacing it.
