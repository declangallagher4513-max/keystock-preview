What you end up owning

Three accounts and a folder of files. All three accounts are in your name, with your email on them and a password only you know.

  • Your domain name. The address people type. Around $20–30 a year, paid by you to the registrar. Nobody else can take it while it is renewed.
  • Your hosting. Where the site actually sits. Free at the size of a one-page site, and it stays free.
  • Your enquiry form. The thing that emails you when somebody fills in the contact form. It sends to your inbox, through an account in your name.
  • The files. The website itself, and your photographs.

There is nothing to renew with us and no account of ours that your site depends on. If you never speak to us again, it stays up.

Before we start

Three things to have on hand before you start. We send you a link and you work through it a step at a time, on your phone, whenever suits you. You can stop part-way and pick it up later. Most people are done inside half an hour.

An email address you will still have in five years

This is the important one. Every account gets built on it, and it is how you prove the domain is yours if anything is ever disputed. A personal address you have had for years is better than a new one made for the business, and far better than one that belongs to an internet provider you might leave.

Your ABN, if you want a .com.au name

Australian rules require whoever holds a .com.au to have an active ABN or ACN connected to the name, and it has to stay active for as long as you hold the domain. A sole trader's personal ABN is fine. We cannot use ours — the rules require it to be the business the name belongs to.

No ABN? Two options, both fine. Applying for one is free and takes about fifteen minutes on the Australian Business Register. Or we use a .com instead, which has no eligibility rules at all and costs about the same.

A card, for the domain

The domain is the one thing with a running cost, and it needs to be on your card so the renewal is yours and never lapses because of us. It is the only payment detail anyone will ask you for.

Your domain name

The address people type to reach you. We check what is available and fill in the form; you pay and confirm.

  1. We find you a name that is free

    Usually your business name and suburb in some combination. We check what is actually free and give you two or three to pick from, so you are choosing rather than hoping.

  2. We fill in the registration

    Your business name, your ABN, your email, your address. You are listed as the owner of the name — that is the part that matters, and it is why we need your details rather than ours.

  3. You pay

    Your card, about $20–30 for the year. Turn auto-renew on while you are there. It is the single most useful switch on this page.

  4. You click the confirmation email

    The registrar emails the address you gave us to check it is real. Click the link. If it does not arrive within a few minutes, look in spam.

The one thing that can really go wrong If the domain lapses, the site goes dark and somebody else can buy your name. That is why auto-renew goes on now and why the card should be one that will not expire and be forgotten. Put a yearly reminder in your phone as well.

Your hosting

Where the site actually lives. Free at this size, and it is an account in your name so we can hand it over and step away.

  1. You open the account, on your email

    You open it yourself, on your own email, and choose the password there and then. We never see it. That one detail is what makes the account yours rather than ours.

  2. You click the confirmation email

    Same as before: they email you to check the address is real.

  3. You let us put the site on it

    One button in your hosting account invites us in as a helper. It lets us upload your site and nothing else, and you can withdraw it from the same screen the moment we are finished. Your password stays yours throughout — we never ask for it, and you should never give it to anybody who does.

  4. We connect your domain and put it live

    This is the fiddly part and it is entirely ours. When it is finished, typing your domain into a phone brings up your site.

Your enquiry form

The thing that emails you when somebody fills in the contact form on your site. Same pattern: we set it up, you confirm and take the password.

  1. You open it on your email, and tell us where enquiries go

    You tell us which address enquiries should land in. It can be different from the one the accounts are built on.

  2. You click the confirmation email

    The third and last one.

  3. We send a test enquiry and you watch it arrive

    We fill in your own contact form and you tell us it landed. You are checking the real thing works rather than taking our word for it, which is the point of doing it now instead of finding out in six months.

  4. You let us connect it to your site

    Third and last.

Changing your site

New hours, a new phone number, a price that has moved, a note that you are shut for a week. Here is how that works today, and what is coming.

Today: you ask, and we quote it

Send us what you want changed. Small text changes are quick and we will tell you the cost before we touch anything — there is no hourly meter running and nothing is done until you say yes.

This is worth being straight about, because it is the one part of the deal that is not "pay once and walk away": the site is yours and costs you nothing to keep running, but us changing it for you is a job like any other.

What counts as a small change?

Wording, hours, phone numbers, prices, adding or removing a service, swapping a photograph. Things that fit the page as it already is.

A new section, a second page, or a different layout is a bigger job and we will say so rather than quietly charging you for one.

Can I edit the files myself?

Yes. You own them, and the site is a single HTML file — anyone who works with websites can edit it, and so can you if you are willing to learn. You do not need our permission and you will not break anything that cannot be put back, because you have the original.

Coming: changing it yourself, from your phone

We are building an editor that will let you change the simple things — hours, phone number, prices — by tapping them on your own page, with no code and no password to remember. It is not finished, and we would rather tell you that than describe something you cannot use yet.

When it exists it will be free for anyone we have built a site for, and it will change nothing about who owns what. Your site will not depend on it: the editor writes changes to your site, and if it were to stop working tomorrow your website would carry on exactly as it is.

Keeping it working

Three things need occasional attention. All three are yours, and none of them takes longer than a minute.

1. The domain renews once a year

Auto-renew handles it as long as the card on file is current. If you get a new card, update it. This is the only one that can actually take your site off the internet.

2. Test the enquiry form every few months

Fill in your own contact form and check it arrives. Silence from a contact form looks exactly the same whether it is broken or nobody is enquiring, which is why it is worth checking rather than assuming.

3. Keep your Google listing right

Your Google Business Profile is worth more than the website for being found, and it is free. Keep the hours accurate, especially at Christmas, and ask happy customers for reviews. If you change your phone number, change it there too.

If something looks wrong

In rough order of how often it actually happens.

The site will not load at all

Nine times out of ten the domain has lapsed. Log in to your registrar and look at whether it is expired — if it is, renewing usually brings the site straight back.

If the domain is fine, check whether it is only you: try it on mobile data with the wi-fi off. If it works there, the problem is your own connection, not the site.

Enquiries have stopped arriving

Send yourself one through the form. If it arrives, they were probably going to spam — find an old one, mark it "not spam", and add the sender to your contacts.

If it does not arrive, log in to the form account and check it has not hit a monthly limit or had the email address changed.

Something on the site is wrong or out of date

Tell us what it should say. Nothing on the page is dangerous to change and we keep every previous version, so putting it back how it was is always possible.

My phone number changed

Send us the new one and we will change it everywhere on the site at once — it appears in several places, including the tap-to-call button.

Change it on your Google Business Profile too. That is separate, it is not something the website can update for you, and more people will find you through it than through the site.

I have lost the password to one of the accounts

Every one of them has "forgot password", and they all send the reset to the email address the account was built on. That is why that address matters more than the passwords do.

Taking it somewhere else

Written down because a promise that you are not locked in is worth nothing unless the exit is documented.

You do not need our permission or our help, and nothing has to be unpicked from our end, because nothing of yours runs through us.

  • To another designer. Give them the logins to the three accounts and the folder of files. That is genuinely all there is.
  • To a different host. Your domain is not tied to your hosting. Point it wherever you like, whenever you like.
  • Away from the website entirely. Cancel the hosting and the form. Keep the domain if you ever want it again — it is cheap, and it stops anyone else using your name.

If you want us to help hand it over, we will, and we will not charge you for that.
